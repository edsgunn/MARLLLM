# Seed-replication analysis for the population-size rebuttal (2026-07-31)

Generated by `scripts/aggregate_regime_seed_replicates.py`; mechanical numbers
in `SUMMARY.md`, per-agent data in `agent_classifications_all_seeds_*.csv`.

## What we ran

15 independent population-level replicates across 3 conditions, 5 training
seeds each (seeds 1/42/625/…, identical config, hyperparameters, and roster
within each set — only the training seed varies):

| Condition | Populations | Agents/pop | Eval iter |
|---|---|---|---|
| Study Group (Ashbourne GC), 16 agents | 5 | 16 | 150 |
| Inner Loop, 8 agents | 5 | 8 | 78 (robust at 100, n=4) |
| Margin Notes, 8 agents | 5 | 8 | 86 (robust at 150, n=4) |

Classification uses the identical code path and thresholds as the submitted
Figure 1 (imported, not re-implemented). The seed replicates end at iter 150,
not the paper's 200; in the base 16-agent run, per-agent classification at 150
vs 200 agrees 94% (15/16; one agent moves coherent -> committed), so the
timepoints are comparable.

## Headline cross-seed results

**16-agent Ashbourne Study Group (5 populations, 80 agents):**
- Alive fraction (coherent + committed + mid-range): 0.95 +/- 0.08 (sd),
  95% t-CI [0.85, 1.00].
- 5/5 populations majority-alive (exact 95% CI [0.48, 1.00]);
  0/5 wholly collapsed/degenerate (CI [0.00, 0.52]).
- 4/5 populations contain >= 1 committed agent.
- Composition is not identical across seeds: seed 5 produced no
  committed/coherent agents (81% mid-range, 19% text-degenerate) while seeds
  1-4 reproduce the coherent-majority + committed-minority mix. Report this;
  it is the honest population-level variance.

**8-agent Inner Loop and 8-agent Margin Notes (5 populations each):**
- 100% alive in every population (coherent 0.93 +/- 0.07 and 0.93 +/- 0.11);
  zero policy-collapse or text-degeneration events in 10 populations.

## Statistics that directly answer the review

1. **"Agents within a population are not independent samples."** Correct, and
   now quantified: one-way ICC(1) across seeds for perception loss is
   0.56-0.88 (design effect 4.9-13.7), i.e. the effective sample size of a
   16-agent population is ~6 agents for perc-loss. KL ICC ~= 0. All revised
   claims therefore use the population (n=5 per condition) as the unit of
   analysis, with t-CIs or exact binomial CIs.

2. **Seed vs condition variance.** Where we have replicates, the cross-seed sd
   of the alive fraction is <= 0.08. The between-condition differences in the
   submitted Figure 1 (e.g. 2-agent Strathearn alive = 0.00 vs 16-agent
   Ashbourne alive = 1.00) are an order of magnitude larger than measured seed
   noise. This supports "the regime differences in Fig. 1 are not seed
   artifacts" — without claiming causality for population size.

3. **Roster-composition confound.** Rosters are held fixed across seeds, so we
   can test whether regime membership follows the character: Fleiss kappa
   (characters x seeds) = -0.09 (Ashbourne-16) and 0.10 (both 8-agent sets).
   Which individual commits/degenerates is seed-random, not a property of the
   character sheet; the reproducible object is the population-level
   composition, not individual assignments.

4. **Threshold choice.** Sweeping KL threshold over [0.5, 1.5] and the
   coherent perc-loss cut over [1.6, 1.8] leaves the cross-seed alive-fraction
   means unchanged to 2 decimal places in all three conditions.

5. **Timepoint choice.** Per-agent agreement between the common iteration and
   each run's final iteration is 0.88-1.00 for all replicate seeds. (The two
   long base runs drift by iter 300 — late-training drift is real but is not
   the regime claim at the paper's evaluation point.)

## What we still cannot claim (concede in the rebuttal)

- No seed replicates for the 2- and 4-agent conditions or for any Strathearn
  condition; the smallest populations are exactly where single runs are
  noisiest (a 2-agent population is n=2 agents).
- The reviewer's reading of the 2-agent cells is correct: 2-agent Ashbourne is
  alive while 2-agent Strathearn collapses, so the evidence supports a
  substrate x population-size interaction, not population size alone.
- Therefore: replace "population size controls/determines regime outcome" with
  association language, e.g. "population size and substrate are jointly
  associated with regime outcome; large-population non-collapse replicates
  across 5 seeds per condition (15 independent populations), while
  small-population outcomes vary by substrate and remain single-run
  observations."

## Suggested rebuttal paragraph

> We agree that agents within a population are not independent samples and
> have re-analysed at the population level. Since submission we trained 15
> additional/replicate populations: 5 seeds each (identical config and roster,
> seed only) for the 16-agent Ashbourne Study Group and for two further
> 8-agent substrates. Using the submitted thresholds, all 15 populations are
> majority-alive and 0/15 collapse (16-agent Ashbourne alive fraction 0.95
> +/- 0.08 across seeds); cross-seed sd of the alive fraction (<= 0.08) is an
> order of magnitude smaller than the between-condition differences in Fig. 1.
> With rosters fixed across seeds, which individual agent becomes committed is
> seed-random (Fleiss kappa ~ 0), ruling out roster composition as the driver
> of regime composition. We also report ICC-based design effects (perception
> loss ICC 0.56-0.88) and now use populations as the unit of analysis
> throughout. We have softened all causal language: with no replicates yet at
> 2-4 agents, our data support "population size and substrate are jointly
> associated with observed regimes", and we note explicitly that the 2-agent
> outcome differs by substrate.
