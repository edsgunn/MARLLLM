You are analysing posts from an online discussion forum. You will be shown a single POST written by one participant, preceded by the posts that came before it (the CONTEXT). Each context post is labelled with its turn_index and the participant who wrote it.

Your task is to identify whether the POST expresses approval or disapproval of something another participant said or did earlier (in the CONTEXT). This is a descriptive labelling task. You are not judging whether anything is right or wrong. Do not assume that approval or disapproval is expected, normal, or desirable here — many posts contain neither, and reporting that is correct.

Apply these definitions literally, not morally:
- "approve": the POST expresses agreement, praise, endorsement, encouragement, thanks, or positive uptake of another participant's specific contribution.
- "disapprove": the POST expresses disagreement framed as correction or criticism, dismissal, mockery, rebuke, or a demand that another participant change their behaviour.
- "neutral": the POST is directed at another participant's behaviour but carries no positive or negative evaluation of it.

If the POST simply continues the discussion, asks a question, builds on earlier content without evaluating it, or makes a new contribution, then it contains no reaction at all — return an empty list. Do not invent a reaction that is not clearly present.

A single POST may contain zero, one, or more reactions (it can react to two different earlier behaviours). Report each reaction you find as one event with these fields:
- "valence": one of "approve", "disapprove", "neutral".
- "intensity": a number from 0.0 to 1.0 for how strongly the reaction is expressed (use 0.0 for neutral).
- "target_turn_index": the turn_index of the specific earlier post being reacted to, or null if the reaction is diffuse / not tied to one post.
- "target_agent_id": the exact name of the participant who wrote the targeted post, or null if diffuse.
- "target_is_self": true if the reaction is directed at the POST author's own earlier contribution, otherwise false.
- "trigger_description": a short, concrete, neutral description of what the targeted participant actually did — the behaviour being reacted to — in your own words. Describe the action, not whether it was good or bad. Do not use evaluative words here.
- "evidence_span": a short quote (at most 160 characters) copied verbatim from the POST that signals the reaction.
- "confidence": a number from 0.0 to 1.0 for how confident you are that this is a genuine reaction.

Output rules:
- Respond with strict JSON only. No prose, no explanation, no markdown, no code fences.
- The response must be exactly an object of the form {"events": [ ... ]}, where each element of the list has the eight keys above.
- If the POST contains no reaction, respond with {"events": []}.
